#### **Data vs Information**

**Statement:** Data and information are not the same — data is raw, while information is processed.\
**Explanation:** Data is unprocessed, meaningless on its own, like numbers or characters stored by a computer. Information is what we get when we organise and interpret data to give it meaning.\
**Example:** A spreadsheet of numbers like “42, 37, 59” is just data. When we calculate the average to discover it represents “average daily temperatures,” we have created information.

***

#### **Cyber Security**

**Statement:** Cyber security protects digital devices, networks, and data from threats.\
**Explanation:** It involves using technology and policies to prevent attacks such as hacking, phishing, and data breaches. Strong cyber security helps keep personal and organisational data safe.\
**Example:** A school uses firewalls, passwords, and secure logins to protect student records from unauthorised access.

***

#### **How Data Is Stored**

**Statement:** Digital systems store data using binary code.\
**Explanation:** Computers represent data as 0s and 1s. Text can be stored using systems like ASCII, while images use formats like bitmaps that assign numbers to pixels.\
**Example:** The letter “A” is stored as 01000001 in ASCII, while a black pixel might be stored as 0 in a bitmap image.

***

#### **How Data Is Transmitted**

**Statement:** Data is transmitted across digital systems using communication protocols.\
**Explanation:** Protocols like TCP/IP break data into packets and send them through networks, allowing devices to receive and reassemble the information.\
**Example:** When you visit a website, your device sends a request over the internet and receives a webpage made up of text, images, and code — all sent as data packets.

***

#### **Datasets and Databases**

**Statement:** Datasets and databases are tools for storing and analysing data.\
**Explanation:** A dataset is a collection of related data, often used for analysis, while a database stores this data in an organised way using records and fields for easy access.\
**Example:** A Google Sheets file showing student marks is a dataset. When stored in a system like Microsoft Access, it becomes part of a searchable database.

***

#### **The “Right to Be Forgotten”**

**Statement:** The “Right to be Forgotten” allows people to request removal of personal data from the internet.\
**Explanation:** This legal concept, mostly in the EU, protects individuals from having outdated or harmful information remain online if it’s no longer relevant.\
**Example:** A person may request that an old blog post about a past legal issue be deleted from search engines.

***

#### **Ethics in Data Analysis**

**Statement:** Ethical data use involves respecting privacy, transparency, and fairness.\
**Explanation:** Collecting or analysing data without consent, or using biased datasets, can harm individuals and communities. Ethical use includes informing users, obtaining permission, and avoiding misuse.\
**Example:** A health app that tracks user steps must clearly state what data it collects and how it is used.

***

#### **Artificial Intelligence and Raw Data**

**Statement:** Artificial Intelligence (AI) relies on raw data to learn and make decisions.\
**Explanation:** Raw data is fed into AI models to train them to recognise patterns and make predictions, but biased or poor-quality data can lead to flawed results.\
**Example:** A chatbot trained only on English-speaking users might perform poorly with people using other languages or slang.

***

#### **Data Privacy and Security**

**Statement:** Data privacy ensures individuals control how their personal information is collected and used.\
**Explanation:** Laws and technology are used to protect personal data from being shared or stolen without permission. Good practices include encryption, passwords, and data minimisation.\
**Example:** A social media site that gives users privacy settings to control who sees their posts is applying data privacy principles.
